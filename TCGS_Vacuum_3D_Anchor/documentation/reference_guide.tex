\section*{Scope and archive coverage}
This guide identifies the 25 works cited in the accompanying manuscript and explains their evidentiary role. Reference numbers match the manuscript. Foundational TCGS works are cited as independent papers, not as undifferentiated compilations.

\textbf{Six of the 25 cited works are supplied as actual PDFs: the central experiment and five complete framework-paper extracts. Nineteen external reference PDFs are not included.} Their texts or full HTML versions were accessible through the browser, but PDF byte downloads into the working filesystem failed. The source archive preserves the two full supplied framework compilations as well. This availability record must not be read as a completed external-PDF collection.

The companion \texttt{reference\_manifest.json} records a versioned PDF target for each missing reference. The supplied downloader validates the signature, page tree, and recognizable title of each received PDF and records a SHA-256 hash. It reports failures rather than saving an HTML error page under a PDF filename. This guide summarizes references; it is not a replacement copy of any article.

\section*{Reading order and evidentiary roles}
For the carrier argument, start with the experiment [1], the quasi-two-dimensional review [5], and the trapped-gas theory [6]. Read the platform precursor [9] for the specific spin-field mapping. The confinement argument should be understood before connecting it to the framework foundations [2--4].

For the fluctuation and measurement argument, the central sources are the experiment [1], Gaussian covariance theory [13], and image-correlation extraction [11]. The Wigner-method review [12] supplies the computational approximation context; the electromagnetic papers [14,15] supply a distinct measurement comparison, not interchangeable data.

For dimensional comparisons, use the Planck analysis [4] with the independent spectral sources [19--22]. The holographic works [23--25] specify the technical claims that must be separated from a finite-width atomic carrier. These comparison references prevent unsupported extensions of the experimental conclusion.
